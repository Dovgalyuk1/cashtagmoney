02

Four hops. None of them need the recipient.

The launcher names the handle. The trader pays the fee. The pipeline does the rest, and the handle owner only shows up once, to confirm a memo.

01 · LAUNCH

Name a handle at launch

Launch on Pons like any other coin, but with one extra field: the $cashtag, @venmo or payment link that should receive the fees. It is written into the coin and cannot be edited afterwards, not even by you.

02 · COLLECT

Fees pile up in USDG

Every buy and sell pays 2% into a vault that belongs to the coin, not to a person. The vault is a plain contract on Robinhood Chain, readable by anyone, drainable by no one.

03 · SWEEP

Vault crosses $5, sweep fires

The USDG bridges to USDC on Base through Across. From there the payout rail buys dollars and sends them to the handle, with the coin ticker in the note. Roughly two minutes end to end.

04 · BURN

The rest buys and burns $CASHTAG

Whatever is not sent to the handle is spent buying $CASHTAG from the open market and sent to the dead address in the same transaction. There is no treasury that holds it in between.

DEFAULT SPLIT OF EVERY FEE
80% to the handle20% buys & burns $CASHTAG

Launchers can push the handle's share up to 90% or down to 50%. The burn side is always what is left. There is no third slice for the protocol.

How a handle claims. The first sweep sends $0.01 to the handle with a six-letter memo. Whoever controls the handle types that memo here once, and every payout after that is automatic. Until the memo is confirmed, funds wait in the vault. After 90 days unconfirmed, the waiting balance is burned as $CASHTAG instead, so nothing sits idle.

Rollout.

Three phases. The boards above go from simulated to live between the first two.

NOW
  • $CASHTAG live on Robinhood Chain via Pons.
  • Desk open: route any handle, generate a launch card, share it.
  • Memo-claim flow finalised for $cashtag and @venmo handles.
AT LAUNCH
  • Launch form opens: name a handle, deploy on Pons in one step.
  • First sweeps fire. Boards switch from simulated to onchain data.
  • Payment links added as a third handle type.
AFTER
  • Split-to-many: one coin, up to five handles, weighted.
  • Recurring handles: a coffee shop's handle can be reused by any coin without a new memo.
  • Public burn ledger with per-coin attribution.
